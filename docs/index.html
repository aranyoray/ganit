01 · Who I am · What I built

A calm, curious math app for kids who learn differently.

GANIT (GenAI Adaptive Numeracy Improvement and Training) is an AI + AR mobile math app built to help children with dyscalculia, ADHD, autism, and math anxiety learn through stories, visuals, and small calm steps. Using Socratic methods and immersive learning, GANIT makes math feel like exploration — not pressure.

It is available as a web app and on the iOS App Store. The Android app is being prototyped.

Made by Aarav Kapoor Grade 5 · Bullis Charter School Spring 2026

Why I built GANIT

1 in 5 children in the US experience arithmetic differences. After interviewing friends and classmates, I heard the same things again and again — "Addition is scary", "Multiplication confuses me", "UGH" said all the time. I wanted to build something that made math feel kind, colorful, and adventurous instead.

04 · The Final Project

What GANIT does today.

Live app: goganit.app (iOS) · web preview: ganit-phi.vercel.app

  • AR mode: place & count 3D spheres, visualise exponents in AR space.
  • GenAI quiz generator: single-digit through advanced arithmetic.

02 · 03 · My Process

From a question to a working app.

The Problem

I interviewed 6 friends about math apps. Friends said "Addition is scary", "Multiplication confuses me", and "UGH" all the time. Existing apps didn't combine generative AI, AR, and story-based mini lessons for learners who struggle.

The Idea

Could bringing tactile and 360° math experiences to every smartphone make math magical? I sketched quest-based learning with big colorful number blocks, calm animations, and adaptive pacing.

The Iterations

Built early prototypes, tested with classmates, iterated on feedback. AR boot time was fixed, the placement UX was redesigned, and a deprecated library was replaced with a supported alternative.

Research-led, kid-tested

  • Literature review: dyscalculia & math anxiety on PubMed, IEEE, Google Scholar.
  • Interviews: 1st & 2nd graders + a 5th-grade friend on the reward system.
  • Build: RealityKit, ARKit, UIKit; random shapes for color-blind accessibility.
  • Feedback: 10+ peers used the iOS app; quantified survey results.
  • Test & iterate: repeated runs on iPad, fixed boot time, deprecated code, added random colors & shapes.
05 · Growth & Learnings

What I learned along the way.

Skills built

  • Coding: Swift, ARKit, RealityKit, GenAI.
  • Research: academic papers, evidence-based design.
  • Leadership: built and ran the GoGanit team.
  • Resilience: many failed prototypes and setbacks.
  • Time management: school + competitions.

Real engineering moments

  • Display library deprecated mid-build → migrated to a supported alternative.
  • AR boot time of 2+ minutes → optimized loading flow to ~20 seconds.
  • AR placement painful → redesigned UX with visual guides.
  • AR objects at wrong scale → fixed coordinate system and radius values.
  • Two big bugs: picture-in-picture (took a week!) and a 20-meter radius oops.
06 · How I used AI

AI as a tool to amplify, not replace.

Tools & resources

  • ChatGPT & Claude — brainstorming features, refining story tone & lesson UX.
  • Gemini — auto-generated 540 questions (30 × 3 topics × 6 levels), filtered & edited.
  • NotebookLM — organising research notes from PubMed, IEEE, Google Scholar.
  • ARKit + RealityKit + ARCore — building the immersive AR math experience.

My AI philosophy

AI should make learning feel calm, personal, and encouraging — especially for children who learn differently. AR should augment both visual and color-blind learners with multi-sensory experiences. Math can feel fun and adventurous when children explore on their own, not because of a preset curriculum that restricts their curiosity.
